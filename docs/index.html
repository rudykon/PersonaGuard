PERSONAGUARD · RESEARCH PROJECT

From Evidence to Action: Auditing Personalization Decisions in HCI Systems

Accurate prediction alone does not establish that a proposed personalization use is justified.

We introduce a route-level audit protocol that connects each proposed use to its evidence, comparator, consequences, and retention or transfer requirements—then records an action and the conditions for review.

Explore the method ↓Read the results ↓Code ↗

Why audit each proposed use?

When should AI personalize? A profile used to explain current performance, change an interface, or support a later encounter needs different evidence for each use. A single “valid” label cannot settle those decisions.

Measurement validity connects interpretation to use; documentation and auditing make evidence and responsibility visible. The remaining task is to connect a specific use, its matched comparator, its consequences, and unresolved requirements to a next action.

RQ1
Can one record structure and rule resolver represent different personalization uses, distinguish their actions, and respond as specified to structural tests and rule removal?
RQ2
What interpretation of the temporal-deviation profile remains warranted within and across videos when measurement conditions change?
RQ3
Under each information condition and comparator, what evidence do content, physiological sensing, and behavioral feedback add—and which actions does it support?

Three contributions

  1. A use-specific audit method linking evidence, actions, responsibility, and review.
  2. A shared rule contract that represents and distinguishes heterogeneous uses.
  3. An empirical case with explicit stopping conditions for interpretation, sensing, deployment, and reuse.

METHOD

Evidence-to-Action Audit Protocol

Specify one use, record human evidence judgments, resolve the ordered rules, and retain an action with its rationale, owner, and review trigger.

Specify the use
State the interpretation, intended action, information available when acting, outcome, and retention or transfer horizon. Changing one can create a new route.
Record the evidence
Separate evidence states from actions. Record the matched comparator, user outcomes, control or reversibility, stability, transfer, and reference coverage.
Resolve an action
Apply the declared priorities. The result can limit use, keep a comparator, request a study, withhold reuse, or permit use within tested limits.
Trace and reconsider
Link each judgment to its source. A human owner reviews the decision and records when new evidence or a changed use requires another review.

People judge evidence and remain responsible for the action. The program checks records and applies declared rules; it does not verify the truth or completeness of the evidence.

Five questions within the protocol

R1Does the conclusion reach beyond who, where, or what was tested?+

A result may depend on the people, setting, or information tested. If broader use is unsupported, limit its interpretation and use to that tested scope.

R2Do added sensors or automatic changes really improve the result?+

Compare against the alternative already tested for this task. When added benefit has not been demonstrated, keep that tested comparator.

R3Did a metric improve, or were user outcomes also evaluated in use?+

A better measurement or stated preference does not establish a benefit in actual use. If that is the only evidence for deployment, first run a preregistered study with users in context.

R4Has long-term reuse been checked for stability, transfer, and population coverage?+

A profile may not remain valid in another session or setting, and its reference data may not cover the relevant people. Missing or incomplete evidence means holding off on long-term storage and reuse elsewhere.

R5Is there direct evidence for this use, with user control or reversibility?+

Reversible personalization also needs direct user-outcome evidence that matches the proposed use. Only when all permission conditions are met and no other rule requires a restriction can it proceed within tested limits.

The next step may be to keep the comparator, gather evidence, limit the scope of use, or permit personalization when conditions are met. Insufficient evidence never means approval by default.

Execution order and permission boundaries+

R4 → R2 → R3 → R1 → R5

The questions above are numbered for explanation, not execution. Rules run in ascending priority order; the first match determines the result. The numeric priorities are 10, 20, 30, 40, and 90, respectively.

  • R1 limits the scope of a result; it does not authorize personalization.
  • R5 must independently satisfy all its permission conditions, with no higher-priority constraint matched.
  • When no rule matches, the program requests the specific missing evidence; it never approves by default.

A correction learned from separate calibration videos reduced error against a reference trace. Does that justify deployment?

  3. Reopen with direct, use-matched evidence and prespecified benefit and harm criteria. Re-run all rules, including the remaining R5 conditions.

EVALUATION

Two evaluation layers, different questions

Protocol evaluation addresses RQ1. Evidence generation in the continuous-affect case addresses RQ2 and RQ3. They share four coded use records, while evaluating different objects.

RQ1 · Protocol evaluation

Four worked use records plus seven records coded by the authors from six HCI papers use one schema and resolver. Structural challenges and single-rule ablations test representation, action discrimination, and the declared responses.

The unit is a coded proposal, not a participant or an independently validated judgment.

RQ2 / RQ3 · Empirical evidence

A secondary analysis of MER-PS covers 24 participants, 15 shared videos, and 360 trials. It studies profile interpretation, optional sensing, signed calibration, and the information needed for each proposed use.

One session, a fixed video library, and one two-dimensional joystick interface. The observations are continuous self-reports on a 1–255 valence–arousal scale.

Validation splits and analysis conditions+

Scalar sensing and cross-video calibration use five outer and four inner participant folds. Content and physiological trajectory analyses hold out both participants and released videos; SAM reconstruction holds out participants while using same-video training reports. References, targets, scaling, and model selection are rebuilt within the applicable training boundary.

Scalar timing-deviation error is in seconds; trajectory error is in joystick label units. Uncertainty and split sensitivity describe development on this release, not an untouched confirmatory cohort.

RESULTS

From protocol behavior to evidence and action

RQ1

Route Discrimination and Structural Conformance

The four worked uses returned four distinct constraint actions. Together with seven external author-coded uses, the same resolver reached all five substantive actions—including five bounded permissions.

The permitted uses were a user-adaptable menu, ability-based generated interfaces, a culturally adaptive website, exposed body-sensing calibration, and conservative menu adaptation. Each permission remains inside its source study’s evaluated boundary.

All structural challenges returned the declared responses. Removing R1–R5 changed 1, 2, 2, 1, and 5 actions respectively; removing R5 turned all five permissions into unresolved evidence requests.

These results support representation and rule execution on these records. They do not establish independent coding validity or improved real-world decisions.

RQ2

Interpretation Boundaries of the Candidate Profile

The profile describes timing differences from reference participants in one session. Its values depend on the reporting interface, alignment estimator, and reference group.

Here, a “profile” is a description calculated from experimental self-report records. It is not an established long-term personal trait, and the reference curve is a comparison report rather than emotional ground truth.

Within a trial, timing alignment on one reporting axis helped the other. The axes share a hand, joystick, task, and clock. Across videos, the supported transfer was only a small constant signed shift; it did not establish a persistent personal timing pattern.

Changing the DTW objective reduced profile-rank agreement to 0.497; changing the reference cohort shifted one profile by up to 0.459 seconds. These dependencies must remain part of the profile’s interpretation.

R1 limits interpretation and use to the evaluated conditions. No repeat-session or cross-interface evidence establishes stable personal attributes.

Additional measurement evidence+

Alternative profile definitions had rank correlations of 0.890–0.965 with the consensus definition. Path-mean normalization increased timing magnitude by 0.783 seconds (95% CI 0.570–0.989). Relative and absolute shared-reference variance fractions were 0.760 and 0.714; these describe sensitivity under dependent, overlapping references.

DISCUSSION

What changes when the use is the unit of review?

Make the decision inspectable

The same evidence can support one use and constrain another. Connecting each judgment to a rule, source, and owner makes the comparison, consequence, and priority open to review. Revising the judgment creates a new decision trace.

Match the comparator to the decision

A completed-trial timing estimate, a prediction before user feedback, and a reconstruction after SAM feedback answer different questions. Information timing is part of the claim, not a detail to omit when comparing errors.

Separate metric improvement, user benefit, and reuse

Lower reference error can identify the next study without justifying deployment. Actual benefit, acceptable degradation, user control, persistence, transfer, and reference coverage each require evidence matched to the intended consequence.

Limits of the present evidence

  • Cases were purposively selected and author-coded after protocol development; there is no independent coding validation or source-author endorsement.
  • Models were developed on one dataset with no untouched confirmatory cohort. The study and primary residual gate were not preregistered.
  • One session and interface, overlapping references, and unavailable demographic and sensor-quality records limit generalization and persistence claims.
  • Offline errors and calculated exposure do not establish live user benefit, harm, burden, privacy, control, or fairness.

What to test next

  1. Independent analysts: code and resolve cases, document disagreements, and measure time, usability, and decision quality.
  2. Prospective interaction study: compare a transparent baseline with reversible adaptation, specifying benefit, harm, burden, and exit criteria first.
  3. Separate cohort and stimuli, dependence-aware uncertainty checks, and repeat-session or cross-interface measurements.

Personalization is a process of making, recording, and revisiting evidence judgments for a specific use; prediction accuracy is only one part of that process.
